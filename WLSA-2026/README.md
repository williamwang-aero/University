# Notes

## Requirements & Strategies

The most important part of entering unis are of course, your grades. Focus on getting good grades first, then worry about other projects. Furthermore, there's something called "Standardized Exams". These exams include SAT and ACT, and are required for most US Unis as an international student. 

## Extracirricular

For extracirriculars, universities don't have a "list" of things required for you to get in. Extracirriculars can be anything: sports, a hobby, volunteering. One of the representatives said a very important part to this: Do what you love, and use it as an advantage for uni. Find out what you love doing, and build on it. Show uni admissionors that you truly love this. Starting a learning log is useful as well.

## Essays

Uni Essays are extremely important. The most important part, however, is reading the question of the essay. One of the representatives (Michigan) stated how many students wrote amazing essays, but they never truly answered the question. Another is do not us AI to help you. Unis want to hear what comes out of you, not out of a computer program. Even if your essay is sh*t, it's still better to write it on your own, rather than copy and paste a whole AI text. If your parents offered to help, say no. Unis, once again, want to hear your own voice. One final thing is to make sure to implement your voice in your essay. Make it emotional, good to read. Put emotions in your words.

## Letters of recommendations

This...is very very helpful for unis. It's probably a must-have now. Letters of recommendations from teachers or coaches are very important for uni entrances. This shows to unis how you can build strong bonds with adults and people in your life. As a representative stated, "not everyone hates you, so find two teachers who doesn't hate you that much, and be nice to them,". This is the only part where you DON'T decide your uni entrance.

## Deadlines/Pathways

Split into two: Early programs and Regular programs. Early programs can be split into early action and early decision. Early action is submitting early, around october, november, while early decision requires a signed form, and it shows how you must committ to what your studying. The early programs are recommended for students who know what they want to do, and they often have a higher admit rate as well. As for regular programs, it has a normal admit rate and doesn't require any committment. It starts around Feb, Jan. Going to the websites of unis you want to go to is very helpful to check.

## Search Process

This is the search process between you and the uni. Ask yourself a question: What exactly do you want to do in the future? Some tips are to understand the uni application beforehand, go to their website to see what unis are looking for, and always use online resources. There's this search engine called "Great Future" search engine which is apparently very good for finding out uni infos.

## Undecided vs Major applications?

When you apply for unis, there's two ways: apply as undecided and apply as a major. Undecided are for students who don't know what they want to study...yet. It's completely okay to choose the option as most students actually don't know that they want to study at uni. The major application is applying for a specific major you want to study. It's for students who know clearly what they want in the future. Remember, applying as a major doesn't tie you to it forever: you still can change after.

## Universify fees/finance

For domestic students, you are eligible for knee-based aid. This is when the uni can lower the price in comparison to family's ability to pay. As for international students, it's usually very hard to get knee-based aid. The only way to apply for scholarships are merit based aid, and extracirriculars. Merit based aids are given to students who have significant achievements than other students. Also, if you do any sports or music, it is possible to get a scholarship if you excel in those fields than others as well.


# Mr Montoya - Questions

What skills do students who perform exceptionally well usually have at/before uni?

Mr Montoya replied by saying how good students at Stanford all are very focused on what they want. They have a clear mindset on what they want to do in the future. This motivates them to stay focused. Students who got accepted into uni were also very focused. 

Do you think working on a big project, and improving it over the years is better, or many small ones?

Mr Montoya replied by saying how he thinks the big project works better. You can show unis the development of your skills throughout the years, and it is more likely to be finished into something way more significant and impressive. He said some students who build lots of small projects usually leave them unfinished before moving onto the next one.

What would you recommend me doing in these next two years?

Mr Montoya gave me a very clear objective: Build connections. Apart from grades and extracirriculars, build connections with other people, like your teachers. As said from before, letters of recommendations are very important. He said how since I loved aerospace, look up to someone who's famous for that field. Contact them, and ask questions. He stated how "building connections is the most important part of not only univeristies, but also for your future."
